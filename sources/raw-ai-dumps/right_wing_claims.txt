Right-wing populist movements and parties share a core set of recurring claims and narratives that frame political and social conflicts around a divide between the "virtuous people" and a detached or corrupt establishment. [1, 2, 3]
Core Claims & Narratives
•	Anti-Establishment and Anti-Elite Rhetoric: The political system, mainstream media, academia, and cultural institutions are accused of ignoring or actively working against the interests of ordinary citizens. [1, 2]
•	Anti-Immigration and "Open Borders" Alarms: High levels of immigration are frequently blamed for economic strain, increased crime, housing shortages, and the erosion of national or cultural identity. Figures like Nigel Farage and international commentators frequently claim that uncontrolled migration makes countries poorer and threatens social stability. [1, 2, 3, 4]
•	National Sovereignty and Protectionism: Globalist institutions (such as the European Union or international agreements) are depicted as surrendering local sovereignty, leading to advocacy for economic protectionism, strict border controls, and neo-nationalism. [1, 2, 3]
•	Cultural Backlash and Traditionalism: Social changes—including LGBTQ+ inclusion, modern gender roles, and progressive education curricula—are often framed as harmful "gender ideologies" that threaten the traditional nuclear family and native culture. [1, 2]
•	The "Forgotten" Native Middle/Working Class: Proponents argue that native-born middle- and lower-income segments are squeezed from above by a detached elite and from below by immigrant or minority groups whose labor or presence is claimed to drive down wages and social standing. [1]
•	Skepticism of Crime and Justice Systems: Populist rhetoric often portrays mainstream legal systems as "pro-crime" or soft on offenders, using high-profile actions or pardons (such as Donald Trump’s pardons of January 6 defendants) to signal defiance against establishment legal norms. [1]
