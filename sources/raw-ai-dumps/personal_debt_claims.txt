Popular claims and common myths about debt often mix helpful financial rules of thumb with outright misconceptions. [1, 2, 3]
Common Claims vs. The Facts
•	Claim: "All debt is bad and will ruin your finances."
	◦	The Reality: Not all debt is the same. Financial educators often distinguish between good debt (such as a mortgage or education loan that can build long-term value or earning potential) and bad debt (such as high-interest credit cards or payday loans used for day-to-day spending). [1, 2]
•	Claim: "If I marry someone, I become responsible for all their debts."
	◦	The Reality: Marriage does not automatically make you liable for your spouse’s pre-existing individual debts. You are only responsible if you co-sign, open a joint account, or live in specific jurisdictions (like certain community property states). [1, 2]
•	Claim: "Paying off a collection or defaulted debt completely erases it from your credit report."
	◦	The Reality: Paying a delinquent account or collection does not immediately delete it. Late payments and collections can generally remain on your credit report for up to seven years, though paying them looks much better to future lenders than leaving them unpaid. [1]
•	Claim: "You can go to prison just for failing to pay off regular debts like credit cards or loans."
	◦	The Reality: In most legal systems, you cannot be jailed simply for being unable to pay standard consumer or credit card debt. Prison risk is extremely rare and typically restricted to specific legal penalties like criminal fines, child maintenance arrears, or willful non-payment of local council taxes or court orders. [1, 2]
•	Claim: "Checking your own credit score lowers it."
	◦	The Reality: Checking your own credit report or score is a "soft inquiry." It has zero negative impact on your credit score. [1, 2]
•	Claim: "Moving to another country makes your debts disappear."
	◦	The Reality: Relocating abroad does not legally wipe out your debt. Creditors and international collection agencies can still pursue balances, and unpaid accounts can follow you or damage your credit standing in your home country. [1, 2]
