In the U.S. consumer market and regulatory landscape, popular claims typically fall into a few major categories where companies face high consumer interest and intense legal or federal scrutiny: [1, 2]
1. "Made in the USA" Claims
•	What they are: Marketing statements asserting that a product is domestically manufactured or assembled. [1]
•	The rule: The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) requires that "all or virtually all" significant parts, processing, and final assembly happen in the United States. [1]
•	Common pitfalls: High-profile false advertising and class-action lawsuits have targeted brands (such as tea companies like R.C. Bigelow or manufacturers like Chemence and Nectar) where raw ingredients or components are sourced or processed abroad. [1, 2]
2. Exaggerated Health, Wellness, and Diet Claims
•	What they are: Product benefits marketed around weight loss, cognitive enhancement, disease prevention, or energy boosts.
•	Notable examples: Past and ongoing FTC enforcement actions have targeted items like 5-Hour Energy, Lumosity (brain training claiming to prevent cognitive decline), Sensa, and various supplement or juice brands making unproven medical or health promises. [1, 2, 3]
3. Iconic Brand Slogans ("Famous Claims")
•	What they are: Long-running strategic taglines that define a brand's identity:
	◦	Nike: "Just Do It"
	◦	Apple: "Think Different"
	◦	Coca-Cola: "Open Happiness"
	◦	L'Oréal: "Because You're Worth It" [1]
